Adventurers are treasure hunters, explorers, troubleshooters, and, very often, first responders. Neophyte adventurers clear giant rats out of basements and locate lost heirlooms, while veteran adventurers foil the plots of mad wizards, destroy portals to the netherworlds, and fight off the rampaging forces of nature.

In addition to being popular among the citizenry because of their utility, adventurers are popular among the city's leadership because the guilds generate significant tax revenue. Inside city limits, adventurers have quasi-legal authority, so they are often given significant deference while in the midst of a quest.

While an average citizen who wants to talk to the mayor might be told to go talk to the city clerk to schedule an appointment that will be repeatedly delayed and rescheduled, an adventurer who wants to alert the authorities to a threat against the city will be ushered directly into the mayor's office.

## Laws of adventuring

Gelbenhügel's city council issues charters to adventuring companies and issues the laws by which they must abide.

- **First**, all adventurers must belong to a chartered adventuring company to operate within the city and surrounding territory.
- **Second**, violence between members of rival companies is prohibited and is punished by expulsion.
- **Third**, members of an adventuring company may lay claim to a newly discovered passage or dungeon by placing their company's mark at the entrance of the dungeon. Once placed, other adventurers are expected to respect the claim, even when the claiming adventurer is no longer physically present.
- **Fourth**, an adventurer may pass through and take treasure from another company's claim, but they are expected to remit half of any recovered treasure to the claiming company.
- **Fifth**, claim disputes can be resolved by any judge or licensed arbitrator.
- **Finally**, if an adventurer saves another adventurer from certain death, they are entitled to half of whatever treasure the other adventurer is carrying.
